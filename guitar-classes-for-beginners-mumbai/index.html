What a complete beginner needs first

Early guitar progress is usually less about memorising dozens of songs and more about getting a few fundamentals right: holding the instrument comfortably, producing clean notes, changing chords without excessive tension, keeping steady rhythm and training the ear to hear what is happening.

Chords and clean fretting

Learn how to place the fingers efficiently, reduce muted strings and move between useful chord shapes.

Rhythm and strumming

Build timing with repeatable strumming patterns instead of relying only on visual chord diagrams.

Playing with singing

The program introduces using the guitar as accompaniment so rhythm and vocals can develop together.

Musical independence

The long-term goal is to understand enough about rhythm, chords and listening to work on songs without permanent dependence on tutorials.

A practical beginner progression

Stage 1 — control the instrument

Posture, tuning, finger placement, basic rhythm and the first usable chord shapes.

Stage 2 — connect chords into music

Chord transitions, strumming consistency and simple song structures. The business's program is intentionally designed to bring practical song-playing into the early lessons instead of postponing it for months.

Stage 3 — understand what you are playing

Develop musical sense around chord relationships, rhythm and listening so that practice becomes less dependent on memorisation.

Stage 4 — become more independent

Apply the foundation to new songs, sing while playing and learn how to figure out more material on your own.

Why home tuition can help a beginner

A beginner benefits from immediate correction because small physical habits—wrist angle, unnecessary pressure, awkward chord fingering or inconsistent timing—can become harder to change later. A private lesson at home also means you practise in the same setup where you normally play between classes.
